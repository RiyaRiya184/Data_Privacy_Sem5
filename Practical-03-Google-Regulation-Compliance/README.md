# Practical 03: Data Protection Regulation Compliance

## 1. Aim

To study the requirements of India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) framework and evaluate the publicly documented privacy practices of Google against selected data protection requirements. The practical also aims to develop a basic compliance plan for an organization handling digital personal data.

---

## 2. Objectives

* To understand the major requirements of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
* To study the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.
* To examine Google's publicly available privacy practices.
* To compare organizational privacy practices with selected regulatory requirements.
* To identify areas that require attention for regulatory compliance.
* To prepare a practical data protection compliance plan.

---

## 3. Organization Selected

**Organization:** Google
**Website:** https://www.google.com/
**Privacy Policy:** https://policies.google.com/privacy

Google provides a wide range of digital services including Search, YouTube, Chrome, Android and Google Account services. Its Privacy Policy explains information collection, purposes of processing, privacy controls, and methods for managing personal information.

---

## 4. Regulations Studied

### Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India's principal legislation concerning the processing of digital personal data. The Act establishes a framework intended to balance individuals' right to protect their personal data with the need to process personal data for lawful purposes.

### Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 provide detailed requirements and implementation mechanisms under the Act. The Rules were notified by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) on 14 November 2025.

The Act and Rules have a phased commencement framework, so compliance requirements must be considered according to the applicable effective dates.

## 5. Methodology

The practical was performed using the following steps:

1. Studied the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
2. Studied the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.
3. Reviewed Google's publicly available Privacy Policy.
4. Identified information categories and purposes described by Google.
5. Examined available privacy controls and user-management features.
6. Compared these practices with selected DPDP requirements.
7. Identified areas requiring further compliance verification.
8. Developed a proposed compliance plan.

This is a desk-based academic assessment. It does not involve penetration testing, unauthorized access, or examination of Google's internal systems.

## 7. Review of Google's Privacy Practices

### 7.1 Information Collection

Google's Privacy Policy describes different categories of information that may be collected when users interact with its services.

Examples include:

* Information provided by users
* Google Account information
* Device and application information
* Information about activity on Google services
* Location-related information
* Information received from third-party sources

### 7.2 Purpose of Data Processing

Google explains that information may be used for purposes including:

* Providing and maintaining services
* Improving services
* Personalizing user experiences
* Security and fraud prevention
* Communication with users
* Advertising and measurement

### 7.3 Privacy Controls

Google provides users with privacy management features. These include tools for reviewing and managing information associated with Google Accounts, Activity Controls and Privacy Checkup.

Users can also manage or delete certain information through their Google Account.

### 7.4 Data Retention

Google provides information about its data retention practices. Different types of information may be retained for different periods depending on factors such as the type of data and the purpose for which it is processed.

Some information can be deleted by users, some is automatically deleted, while certain information may be retained for longer where necessary.

## 9. Compliance Gaps Requiring Further Verification

The following points cannot be conclusively established through a public website review alone:

1. Exact internal data-processing records maintained for Indian users.
2. Internal access-control configurations.
3. Complete security architecture and technical safeguards.
4. Internal incident-response procedures.
5. Contracts and arrangements with all data processors.
6. Exact retention schedules for every category of personal data.
7. Internal procedures used to satisfy every applicable requirement of Indian data protection law.

Therefore, these areas are recorded as **requiring further verification** rather than being classified as confirmed violations.

## 10. Proposed Compliance Plan

### Step 1 — Data Inventory

Create an inventory of all personal data processed.

The inventory should identify:

* Data category
* Source of data
* Purpose of processing
* Storage location
* Retention period
* Parties receiving the data

### Step 2 — Privacy Notice

Provide clear and understandable information to users about:

* What data is collected
* Why it is collected
* How it is used
* Applicable user rights
* How users can contact the organization

### Step 3 — Consent Management

Where consent is the applicable basis for processing:

* Obtain consent appropriately.
* Record consent.
* Provide mechanisms for withdrawal where applicable.
* Maintain evidence of consent management.

### Step 4 — Security Controls

Implement appropriate technical and organizational safeguards, including:

* Access control
* Authentication
* Encryption where appropriate
* Security monitoring
* Secure software development
* Regular security assessments

### Step 5 — Data Retention

Establish retention schedules for different categories of personal data.

Data should not be retained indefinitely without a valid reason.

### Step 6 — User Rights Management

Create processes for receiving and responding to applicable requests relating to personal data.

Requests should be tracked and handled within applicable legal requirements.

### Step 7 — Breach Response

Maintain a documented incident-response procedure covering:

1. Detection
2. Containment
3. Investigation
4. Risk assessment
5. Required notifications
6. Remediation
7. Documentation
8. Post-incident review

### Step 8 — Grievance Management

Provide a clear mechanism through which users can raise privacy-related complaints or concerns.

### Step 9 — Regular Audits

Conduct periodic reviews of:

* Privacy policies
* Data inventories
* Access controls
* Retention schedules
* Security safeguards
* Third-party data processing
