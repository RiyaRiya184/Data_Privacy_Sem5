# Practical 01: Data Privacy Audit of Samarth Portal

## 1. Aim

To conduct a data privacy audit of the Samarth e-Gov Student Portal of the University of Delhi, identify potential privacy risks associated with student information, and suggest measures to improve data protection and privacy.

## 2. Objectives

* To understand the role of data privacy in university ERP systems.
* To examine the publicly accessible Samarth Student Portal.
* To identify categories of personal and academic data that may be processed.
* To review visible privacy-related information and account security features.
* To identify potential privacy risks and recommend suitable mitigation measures.

## 4. Introduction

Data privacy refers to the appropriate collection, processing, storage, and sharing of personal information. Educational institutions handle sensitive student information, including identity details, contact information, enrolment records, and academic data.

The Samarth e-Gov platform is used by higher education institutions to support administrative and student-related services. Since university portals may process personal and academic information, evaluating their privacy practices is important for understanding potential risks to students.

This practical focuses on a preliminary privacy audit of the University of Delhi's Samarth Student Portal.

## 5. Scope of the Audit

The audit focuses on the following areas:

* Publicly accessible portal pages
* Login interface and visible authentication features
* Potential categories of student information
* Availability of privacy notices and support information
* Potential risks related to personal data protection
* Recommendations for improving privacy practices

**Note:** This is a preliminary, non-invasive audit. It does not involve penetration testing, unauthorized access, or inspection of internal server configurations.

## 6. Audit Methodology

The following steps are used to conduct the audit:

1. Open the official Samarth Student Portal.
2. Examine the publicly accessible homepage and login interface.
3. Identify visible login fields and account recovery options.
4. Look for privacy policies, terms of use, and relevant support information.
5. Identify categories of personal information that may be processed by the portal.
6. Evaluate potential privacy risks associated with student information.
7. Prepare a risk assessment and suggest mitigation measures.
8. Document the findings using observations and screenshots.

## 8. Potential Privacy Risks

The following risks are considered during the audit. They are potential risks associated with student information systems and are not confirmed vulnerabilities in the Samarth Portal.

### 8.1 Unauthorized Account Access

If student login credentials are compromised, unauthorized individuals may gain access to personal or academic information.

### 8.2 Excessive Data Collection

Collecting more personal information than necessary for a particular service may increase privacy risks.

### 8.3 Lack of Privacy Transparency

If privacy notices do not clearly explain data collection, processing, retention, and sharing, students may not fully understand how their information is handled.

### 8.4 Improper Access to Student Records

Inadequate access restrictions could expose student information to individuals who do not require it for their official responsibilities.

### 8.5 Account Recovery Risks

Weak account recovery procedures may create opportunities for unauthorized account access.

### 8.6 Excessive Data Retention

Retaining personal information longer than necessary may increase the consequences of a potential data breach.

## 10. Recommendations

Based on the potential risks identified, the following measures are recommended:

1. **Strong Authentication:** Encourage strong passwords and implement appropriate account protection mechanisms.
2. **Data Minimization:** Collect only the personal information necessary for the intended academic or administrative service.
3. **Privacy Transparency:** Provide a clear privacy notice explaining data collection, processing purposes, retention, and sharing.
4. **Role-Based Access Control:** Restrict access to student records according to official responsibilities.
5. **Secure Account Recovery:** Use reliable account recovery procedures to reduce the risk of account takeover.
6. **Data Retention Policies:** Establish clear retention and deletion policies for student information.
7. **Privacy Awareness:** Educate students about protecting credentials and avoiding the public sharing of sensitive academic information.
